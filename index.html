THE INVESTIGATION

What are we actually trying to prove?

?

Across three defined NFC test distances, what first-attempt update success rate and end-to-end update latency does a standalone SwingTag prototype achieve, and how much energy does one display update consume?

Intended outcome

Produce one working prototype, measure update reliability, latency and energy use, and use those measurements to estimate battery life for hourly and daily update schedules.

Why the power work matters

An e-paper display holds an image without continuous power, but an update still needs a short burst of energy. Our circuit investigation asks whether energy can be collected, stored, gated and delivered cleanly enough to support the MCU and eventually the display.

CURRENT LAB OBSERVATIONThe 4700 µF capacitor is charging in approximately 10–15 seconds in the present setup.

This is a preliminary observed range, not yet a fully characterised result. We still need to record the starting and ending voltages, reader distance, alignment and repeat count.

WHY 4700 µF?

It turns the project question into a measurable energy test.

We will charge this capacitor from the NFC harvesting path, release its stored energy into the regulated STM32 and e-paper load, and determine whether one complete update succeeds.

Can the source charge storage?

We began with an illustrative 3.3 V source and 1 kΩ source resistance. In that model, the selected 4700 µF capacitor reached 3.0 V in about 11.6 s. The 0.47 F capacitor was retained only as a larger-storage comparison.

    ENGINEERING INTEGRITY

    Measured, simulated and assumed are not the same thing.

    MEASURED

    Laboratory evidence

    Reader distance, update success, update latency, actual VEH, load current and display-update energy belong here once measured on hardware.

    SIMULATED

    Model behaviour

    LTC1540 thresholds, charge/discharge cycles, MOSFET control and load sensitivity are currently being explored in LTspice.

    ASSUMED

    Temporary design inputs

    The 3.3 V / 1 kΩ source and some MCU load states are screening assumptions. They must not be presented as measured NFC performance.
