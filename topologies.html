Energy-harvesting topologies

Four architectures compared by startup behaviour, energy accumulation and implementation complexity.

TOPOLOGY A

Direct rectified harvesting

Antenna→Harvester→Load

Strengths

Few components, small area and low static overhead.

Limitations

The load directly disturbs the harvested rail. Brownout and repeated startup are likely when available power is marginal.

TOPOLOGY B

Storage + regulator

Harvester→Storage→Regulator→Load

Strengths

Stores energy and improves voltage quality.

Limitations

The converter may start too early and repeatedly drain an undercharged reservoir.

TOPOLOGY C

Storage + supervisor

Harvester→Storage→Supervisor→Load

Strengths

Provides a defined enable decision and reduces premature loading.

Limitations

Adds component cost, quiescent current and threshold-selection constraints.

PROPOSED SWINGTAG DESIGN

Discrete automatic power path

ST25→4700 µF VSTORE→Threshold + PMOS→Buck-boost→STM32 + e-paper

Why investigate it?

It accumulates energy before enabling the load and aims to prevent the MCU and display from starting during an under-energy condition.

Current limitation

Version 1 switched automatically but its practical threshold was governed strongly by PMOS behaviour. Version 2 is being developed for a sharper decision.

DESIGN DECISION

The threshold stage decides. The regulator regulates.

Separating these functions is essential: the monitor waits for sufficient stored energy, the high-side switch connects the load path, and only then does the buck-boost converter establish the 3.3 V rail.
